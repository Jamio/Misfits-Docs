Emergency procedure

Do not try to solve everything at once. Make the scene safe, stop deterioration, then hand the casualty to a medic.

  1. Secure and move. Suppress the threat and drag or carry the casualty into cover.
  2. Check response, pulse and bleeding. No pulse means cardiac arrest. Heavy bleeding must be controlled immediately.
  3. Control major bleeding. Apply tourniquets to bleeding limbs when speed matters, then bandage every wound.
  4. Open the airway and check breathing. Use a manual manoeuvre or an Airway Tube if obstructed. Start CPR if the patient has no pulse.
  5. Call a medic. Blood replacement, surgical treatment and definitive recovery are medic jobs in our setup.
  6. Finish the basics. Remove tourniquets after the limb is bandaged, splint fractures, control pain, and keep reassessing.

For non-medics: stop bleeding, maintain the airway, perform CPR when required, and communicate. You are not expected to rebuild the casualty alone.

The ACE medical menu

Open the medical menu with H, or through ACE interaction. Confirm the patient's name before treating. Select a body region on the figure, then choose the appropriate tab and action.

Body colours

  • White: no displayed wound.
  • Blue: bandaged wounds or bruising.
  • Yellow to red: open wounds, increasing in severity.

A blue body part is not necessarily finished: bandaged wounds may still need stitching.

Menu sections

  • Triage: treatment history and previous medication.
  • Examine: response, pulse, blood pressure and Misfits breathing checks.
  • Bandage: wounds, tourniquets, fractures and surgical treatment.
  • Medication: injectors, tablets and IV fluids.
  • Advanced: CPR and PAK treatment.
  • Airway/Breathing: the Misfits respiratory actions.

Medication is normally applied to an arm or leg. A tourniquet prevents medication and IV fluid from circulating out of that limb until it is removed. Check the triage log before giving another dose.

What makes a patient unconscious?

ACE does not use a single “revive health bar.” Trauma, blood volume, pain, heart rate, blood pressure, cardiac arrest and medication all interact. The Misfits breathing system adds airway status, breathing and SpO₂ to that recovery loop.

Bleeding and blood volume

Open wounds continuously remove blood. Bandaging stops the active bleed; IVs replace lost volume over time. A badly depleted patient may remain unconscious even after every wound is closed.

Pain and trauma

Pain reduces effectiveness and can contribute to unconsciousness. Bandaging controls bleeding but does not necessarily remove all underlying trauma.

Cardiac arrest

No pulse or zero blood pressure is not fixed by opening the airway. CPR buys time and creates opportunities for circulation to return; the original cause still needs treatment.

Stable does not mean awake

Once the dangerous problems are corrected, ACE periodically checks whether an unconscious patient can wake. Epinephrine increases the opportunity for those checks; it is not a guaranteed wake-up button and cannot compensate for catastrophic blood loss, cardiac arrest, an obstructed airway or severe hypoxia.

Advanced treatment

Bandage, then stitch

Bandaging turns an open wound into a closed but potentially temporary repair. A medic must use the surgical treatment on bandaged wounds to make that repair permanent. Bruises do not need stitching and do not block the PAK.

CPR

Use CPR on a patient in cardiac arrest. CPR is not useful simply because somebody is unconscious. Keep the airway clear, stop blood loss, replace volume where required, and use the diagnostic information rather than administering random medication.

Our PAK rule

The PAK action remains hidden until the patient meets the configured requirements. In the full workflow this means no open bandageable wounds, all bandaged wounds stitched, required fractures treated, and the patient sufficiently stable. If the action is absent, something remains unresolved.

Moving casualties

Dragging starts quickly but is slow. Carrying takes longer to begin but moves faster. The Misfits stretcher can transport a casualty and acts as a medical treatment location when the relevant settings permit it.

Airway, breathing and SpO₂

Misfits Medical provides a compact KAT-style respiratory loop without oxygen tanks, pneumothorax, chest drains or a full respiratory simulation.

  1. Check Airway. Establish whether the airway is clear or obstructed.
  2. Clear or maintain it. Extend/turn the head, use the recovery position, or insert an Airway Tube.
  3. Check Breathing. The result is breathing normally, shallow breathing, or not breathing.
  4. Use Pulse Oximeter. Read the patient's SpO₂ when an exact value matters.
  5. Treat circulation. A clear airway cannot make a pulseless patient breathe; CPR and normal ACE stabilisation are still required.

Manual manoeuvres

Head extension or turning is fast, free and temporary. Recovery position is a more sustained hands-free measure for an unconscious patient who is breathing and can safely be left positioned. Movement or deterioration may require reassessment.

Airway Tube

The reliable item-based method of clearing and maintaining the airway. It replaces several overlapping KAT devices with one easy-to-understand treatment.

Prolonged failure to breathe lowers SpO₂. Severe hypoxia prevents consciousness even when other ACE vitals look acceptable. An airway can be clear while the patient is not breathing if circulation or their wider medical state cannot sustain respiration.

Why is the patient not waking up?

  • Are any wounds still open or actively bleeding?
  • Have they lost enough blood to require IV replacement?
  • Do they have a pulse and non-zero blood pressure?
  • Is the airway clear, are they breathing, and has SpO₂ recovered?
  • Are tourniquets still blocking medication or fluids?
  • Have they received too much morphine or conflicting medication?
  • Have bandaged wounds been stitched and fractures treated?
  • Have you allowed time for IVs, medication and ACE wake-up checks to take effect?

When in doubt: stop adding drugs, check the triage log, repeat the vital checks, and work the problem in order.
