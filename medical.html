Guide · ACE Medical

ACE / Misfits Medical

A complete player reference for ACE Medical, our custom settings, and features added by the Misfits Aux mod.

Misfits operations use ACE Medical as their foundation. Our Aux mod adds custom treatments, stricter definitive-care rules, and a lightweight airway and breathing system. The aim is to add depth for medics without making ordinary first aid intrusive or unnecessarily complicated.

How to use this guide: select a tab below to browse each part of the system. This introduction and the tabs remain visible while the guide beneath them changes.

Start here

The basic treatment loop

ACE Medical can be intimidating at first, but the most important priorities are easy to remember. You do not need to understand every number before you can keep somebody alive.

  1. SecureSuppress the threat and move the casualty into cover. Somebody should protect you while you work.
  2. TriageConfirm the patient is alive. Check bleeding, airway, breathing, pulse and obvious fractures.
  3. Stop bleedingTourniquet severe limb wounds when speed matters, then bandage every open wound.
  4. Manage the airwayClear an obstruction with a manual manoeuvre or use an Airway Tube. Check breathing and SpO₂.
  5. Restore circulationStart CPR if there is no pulse. Medics should replace lost blood volume and manage medication.
  6. Finish treatmentRemove tourniquets, splint fractures, stitch bandaged wounds and use the PAK when eligible.

Non-medics

Your job is not to fully heal the casualty by yourself. Get them out of immediate danger, slow or stop deterioration, and hand them to a medic.

Medics

Organise the treatment rather than trying to do everything alone. Give nearby players clear jobs: CPR, bandaging, security, carrying equipment or preparing evacuation.

Remember: an unconscious casualty may have several problems at once. Fixing one value does not guarantee that they will wake up immediately.

Interface

Using the ACE Medical Menu

Open the medical menu with H, or through ACE interaction. Confirm the patient's name before treating, select a body region on the figure, then choose the appropriate tab and action.

Body colours

  • White: no displayed wound.
  • Blue: bandaged wounds or bruising.
  • Yellow through red: open wounds of increasing severity.

A blue body part is not necessarily finished: bandaged wounds may still reopen until stitched.

Select the correct body part

Most actions are contextual. Bandages apply to the wounded region; medication and IVs are normally given through an arm or leg; airway actions use the head. If an action is missing, check your selected body part first.

Trauma

Damage, wounds and fractures

ACE does not use a single visible health bar. Incoming damage creates injuries on individual body parts. The wound's type, size and location determine how quickly it bleeds and how difficult it is to close. Trauma also produces pain, may fracture limbs, and can contribute to unconsciousness or cardiac arrest.

Open wounds

Avulsions, velocity wounds, punctures, cuts, lacerations, abrasions and crush injuries have different bleeding and bandaging characteristics. Treat the patient in front of you rather than trying to memorise every coefficient.

Bruises

Bruises represent trauma but do not bleed, cannot be bandaged or stitched, and deliberately do not block our PAK. The PAK is what finally removes them.

Pain

Pain reduces effectiveness and can contribute to unconsciousness. Treat it after immediate threats such as bleeding, airway failure and cardiac arrest.

Fractures

Fractured limbs reduce mobility or weapon handling. Apply a splint once bleeding is controlled. Untreated fractures block the PAK under our normal settings.

Bandaging, reopening and stitching

A bandage closes a wound and stops that wound bleeding, but it is a temporary repair. Movement and time can allow a wound to reopen. A medic uses the Surgical Kit and sutures to make bandaged wounds permanent; sutures are consumed as wounds are stitched.

Tourniquets: immediately stop every wound on an arm or leg. Use them to buy time, bandage beneath them, then remove them promptly and recheck the limb. They cause increasing pain and prevent medication or IV fluid from circulating out of that limb.

Circulation

Bleeding, blood volume and vital signs

A healthy patient begins with approximately 6 litres of blood. Every open wound removes volume over time. Large wounds and multiple wounds bleed faster, and continued blood loss lowers blood pressure until the patient becomes unconscious, enters cardiac arrest or dies.

How to slow or stop bleeding

  1. Tourniquet catastrophic limb bleeding. This instantly pauses all bleeding from that limb.
  2. Bandage every open wound. A partly treated body region may still contain another bleeding wound.
  3. Use TXA appropriately. It modestly reduces blood loss for a limited time; it never replaces bandaging.
  4. Recheck the casualty. Bandaged wounds can reopen until stitched.
  5. Replace lost volume. Medics use blood, plasma or saline IVs; fluids do not close wounds.

Heart rate

Heart rate changes through trauma and medication. Morphine lowers it; epinephrine raises it. Extreme values contribute to unconsciousness. Check the triage log rather than fighting one drug with repeated doses of another.

Blood pressure

Blood pressure reflects both heart activity and circulating volume. A heart rate can look acceptable while pressure remains dangerously low because the patient has lost too much blood.

Cardiac arrest

No pulse means CPR. CPR creates an opportunity for the heart to restart, but it cannot compensate for uncontrolled bleeding or critically low volume. Keep treating the underlying problems.

Waking up

Once dangerous problems are corrected, ACE periodically checks whether an unconscious patient can recover. Epinephrine improves those opportunities but is never a guaranteed wake-up button.

Our stability rule: the default PAK requirement is roughly 85% blood volume, or 5.1 L, alongside stable circulation and the other conditions explained under Treatments & Equipment.

Respiration

Airway, breathing and SpO₂

Misfits Medical provides a compact KAT-style respiratory loop without oxygen tanks, pneumothorax, chest drains or a full respiratory simulation. Airway, breathing and circulation are separate: a clear throat cannot make a pulseless patient breathe.

How airways become blocked

Unconscious patients can develop an obstruction or full occlusion. Chemical exposure can also interfere with the airway. A blocked airway prevents effective breathing and causes blood oxygen to fall.

What SpO₂ means

SpO₂ represents the oxygen carried in the patient's blood. Breathing maintains it. Shallow or absent breathing reduces it over time; values below 90% are dangerous and can prevent the patient from being considered stable enough to wake.

  1. Check AirwayFind out whether it is clear, obstructed or occluded.
  2. Open it manuallyHead extension or head turning can quickly clear an obstruction, but the benefit is temporary.
  3. Maintain itThe recovery position provides a more sustained manual solution while the patient remains positioned.
  4. Secure itA medic's Airway Tube is the strongest and most durable intervention and improves oxygen recovery.
  5. Check BreathingThe result is breathing normally, shallow breathing or not breathing.
  6. Measure SpO₂Use the Pulse Oximeter for an accurate percentage, then continue treating circulation and the cause.

If the airway is clear but they are not breathing: check pulse, blood pressure, blood volume and cardiac arrest. Continue CPR when pulseless while keeping the airway managed.

Reference

Treatments and equipment

CAT

Tourniquet

Immediately stops limb bleeding. Temporary, painful and blocks medication/IV circulation through that limb.

Rx

Morphine, painkillers and epinephrine

Morphine provides strong pain relief and lowers heart rate. Painkillers are gentler and slower. Epinephrine raises heart rate and helps eligible patients recover; none replaces blood or closes wounds.

IV

Blood, plasma and saline

Restore circulating volume over time and are restricted to medics in our settings. Use smaller bags when only a small replacement is needed.

SK

Surgical Kit, sutures and PAK

The Surgical Kit consumes sutures to permanently close wounds. The PAK provides definitive treatment but only appears when our requirements are met.

TXA Injector

A medic-only treatment that modestly reduces bleeding for a limited time. It does not bandage, stitch or replace blood.

UA

Universal Antagonist

A medic-only emergency reset that clears the patient's active medication burden. Medics may self-administer it.

100

Emergency Fluid Bag

A 100 mL emergency volume stopgap usable by non-medics. It deliberately cannot replace proper medic treatment.

Pulse Oximeter and Airway Tube

The oximeter measures SpO₂; the Airway Tube reliably clears and maintains an airway.

Medical Aid Pack

A deployable persistent supply case. Deployment takes time and repacking preserves its remaining contents.

▰

Misfits Stretcher

Moves casualties, folds for recovery and counts as a medical facility for permitted treatments.

When does the PAK appear?

With our default settings, the patient must be alive and conscious, no longer bleeding, have stable circulation and sufficient blood volume, have every bandageable wound bandaged and stitched, and have every fracture splinted. Bruises are ignored because the PAK is the only way to remove them. The PAK must also be used at an allowed medical facility or vehicle.

Practical guidance

Procedures, teamwork and troubleshooting

Call the medic early

Report who is down, where they are, whether the area is safe, and the immediate problem. Do not wait until you have exhausted your own supplies.

Use extra hands

Anyone can bandage, perform CPR, carry a casualty or provide security. A medic should direct priorities and reserve their attention for advanced treatment.

Moving casualties

Dragging begins quickly but moves slowly. Carrying takes longer to start but moves faster. Use the stretcher for longer movement and treatment at a medical position.

Reassess constantly

Wounds reopen, tourniquets remain fitted and medication takes time to act. Recheck bleeding, pulse, pressure, breathing and treatment history after each major step.

Common problems

The PAK action is missing

Check consciousness, bleeding, circulation, blood volume, stitched wounds, splinted fractures and whether you are at a permitted facility. Bruises are not blockers.

A treatment is not listed

Select the correct body part, confirm that you carry the item, check your medical permission level and verify that the patient's condition makes the action valid.

The patient remains unconscious

Work through every system rather than repeatedly adding epinephrine: bleeding, volume, pulse, pressure, airway, breathing, SpO₂, pain and medication history.

Treatment appears stuck

Stop competing interactions, allow the current progress action to finish and keep both people in a valid position. If it remains broken, tell Zeus and retain the client RPT.

Do not load Misfits Airway alongside KAT Airway. The Misfits system is our compact alternative.
